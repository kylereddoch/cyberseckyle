I have been thinking a lot lately about this blog, the things I publish here, and how I share them with everyone afterward.

I enjoy writing. Sometimes I am explaining a cybersecurity issue that I believe deserves more attention. Other times I am documenting a technical problem I had to solve, sharing something personal, or simply collecting the highlights from my week.

Writing helps me organize my thoughts, document what I have learned, and hopefully help someone else along the way.

Still, publishing something is only part of the process.

I also want to make sure what I am creating is genuinely useful to the people who take the time to read it.

## I would like your honest feedback

I am looking for some constructive feedback about my blog posts and how I share them.

Is there anything I could be doing differently to make my posts more engaging, useful, or worth sharing with others?

I am not necessarily trying to chase algorithms, manufacture engagement, or turn every article into clickbait. That is not what I want this site to become.

However, I also know that good information can easily get overlooked when the title does not connect, the introduction takes too long, the post is difficult to scan, or the article is not shared in a way that gives people a reason to open it.

There is always room to improve.

## What would make you more likely to read?

I would especially appreciate feedback on things such as:

- The topics I write about
- The length of my articles
- The balance between technical details and plain-language explanations
- My article titles and descriptions
- The formatting and readability of the site
- The images used with my posts
- How I introduce and share new articles on Mastodon
- Whether you prefer tutorials, commentary, personal posts, or cybersecurity analysis
- Anything that makes you stop reading or decide not to open a post

You do not have to answer every question or provide a detailed critique. Even something simple like, "I would like to see more practical tutorials," or, "Your posts are sometimes longer than I have time to read," would be helpful.

## What would make a post worth sharing?

Reading and sharing are also two different things.

You might enjoy an article but not feel that it is something you would send to a coworker, boost on Mastodon, or share with someone who needs help.

That makes me curious about what turns a useful article into a shareable one.

Is it a strong practical takeaway? A shorter format? A checklist? A better headline? More original analysis? A clear explanation of why the subject matters?

I have my own opinions about what makes content valuable, but I am also looking at it from the perspective of the person who wrote it. Readers may see things that I completely miss.

## I want this site to remain mine

While I am asking for feedback, I do not intend to remove the personality from this site or turn it into a generic cybersecurity content machine.

This is still my personal corner of the internet.

I want to continue writing about the things that interest me, documenting lessons from my work in IT and cybersecurity, and sharing occasional pieces of my life outside of technology.

The goal is not to appeal to everyone. The goal is to communicate better with the people who are interested in what I have to say.

## Let me know what you think

You can respond through the Mastodon discussion connected to this post, send me a message, or reach out through my contact page.

Honest and constructive feedback is welcome.

What would make you more likely to read, respond to, or share one of my posts?
