## The vibe this year

This year was equal parts:

- shipping practical security writing (for real humans, not just auditors)
- building my personal site like it is a living lab
- grinding through school work while keeping one foot in the real world of MSP and security ops
- learning, tweaking, breaking things, fixing them, then writing down what I wish I’d known two weeks earlier

## The Big Move: Switching to Eleventy

The most significant change this year was [moving my website to Eleventy](/blog/from-start-to-finish-moving-my-blog-to-eleventy-github-pages/). It was one of those changes that seems “just technical” until you feel the difference in day-to-day writing and maintenance.

- Content became easier to structure and reuse
- Layouts and partials stopped being copy-paste chaos
- Collections and tags made the site feel like a real system
- I could iterate faster without breaking the whole house

That move also quietly pushed me toward better habits, especially internal linking and organizing posts so future-me does not hate present-me.

## A New Mastodon Home

As some of you may know, [Cliff](https://infosec.exchange/@cliffwade) and I use to run our own Mastodon instance. *AllThingsTech* was a great place and we enjoyed having a space where we could have like minded people talk about all things tech with the added bonus of what the fediverse is all about. Unfortunately, it was a lot of work to maintain and we had to let it go.

In comes [Infosec.exchange](https://infosec.exchange), a Cybersecurity and Infosec focus instance ran by [Jerry](https://infosec.exchange/@jerry). It's a wonderful community and one that is right up my alley. I've been enjoying all the wonderful converstations and new people I have met. It's been a great place to share my thoughts and learn from others.

## School: the grind that paid off

School work was the steady drumbeat in the background this year. A lot of it revolved around project management fundamentals that sound boring until you realize they are basically incident response for humans:

- defining scope so the project does not mutate into a monster
- setting evaluation criteria so success is measurable, not vibes
- risk identification and risk response planning
- stakeholder management (translation: getting people aligned without starting a small war)

The best part is that I got to constantly tie coursework back to what I already do in IT and security. That made it feel less like homework and more like sharpening tools I actually use.

I only have 3 class left, 15 weeks, and I will be done with school! 🎉

## Community moments that stuck with me

A bunch of my best ideas came from conversations, especially the ones where someone challenged me with a genuine question like:

- [“Cool, but what about fingerprinting?”](https://social.vivaldi.net/@djbiker/115702185904707472)
- [“Does this cover what uBlock and uMatrix do?”](https://gamepad.club/@FibroJedi/115701956966535823)
- [“Why do cookie banners still pop up here but not there?”](https://social.vivaldi.net/@djbiker/115702185904707472)

Those questions pushed me to write more clearly, test more assumptions, and stop treating “privacy” like a single feature you toggle on.

## Personal wins (the quiet kind)

Not everything worth celebrating shows up in a GitHub graph.

This year had plenty of those smaller “life maintenance” wins:

- showing up even when motivation was missing
- keeping curiosity alive when everything felt noisy
- building routines that support my brain instead of fighting it
- making time for fun, even if it was just tinkering with the site or disappearing into a game for a bit

The older I get, the more I respect the boring wins. Those are the ones that keep you upright.
