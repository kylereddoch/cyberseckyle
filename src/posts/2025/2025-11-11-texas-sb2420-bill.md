Texas just enacted **[SB 2420](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/pdf/SB02420F.pdf)**, the “App Store Accountability Act,” which imposes **[age-verification](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/analysis/html/SB02420H.HTM)** and **[parental-consent](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=2ezb6jhj)** duties on app stores and developers. On paper, the goal is to protect kids. In practice, it creates large new targets of sensitive identity data and forces workflows that many orgs are not prepared to defend.

This post unpacks what SB 2420 actually requires, how it interacts with other Texas age-verification laws, and what it means for organizations that care about security and PII hygiene.

## What SB 2420 does

SB 2420 **[adds Chapter 121 to the Texas Business and Commerce Code](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/billtext/pdf/SB02420F.pdf)**

Highlights:

- Creates **mandatory age-verification** for app stores and developers, along with **parental consent** for minor accounts. The law defines “age categories,” assigns them at the app-store level, and expects developers to verify the category for each user and whether parental consent exists for minors ([bill analysis](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/analysis/html/SB02420H.HTM); [Apple developer update](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=2ezb6jhj)).
- Ties the verification system to app-store provided data and prescribes compliance hooks, violations, and enforcement under Texas **[deceptive trade practices](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/analysis/html/SB02420H.HTM)**.
- Applies beyond the narrow category of adult content. SB 2420 regulates **general app distribution** and developer obligations, not just pornography sites ([Legislative tracking summary](https://legiscan.com/TX/bill/SB2420/2025)).

Separate from SB 2420, Texas also has a **pornography-site age-verification law** that the **[U.S. Supreme Court allowed to stand in June 2025](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/us-supreme-court-poised-rule-challenge-texas-age-check-online-porn-2025-06-27/)**, shaping a judiciary more willing to uphold aggressive age checks ([additional coverage](https://apnews.com/article/12a73197796fe8c4bef0d888259543cf)).

Tech platforms have already indicated they will comply with SB 2420, while **[warning about privacy and safety risks](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=btkirlj8)** of doing so at scale.

## The cybersecurity and PII problem

From a defender’s perspective, SB 2420 pushes the ecosystem toward **identity-heavy gating** for activities that were previously pseudonymous or low-friction. That shift has specific risks:

1) **Bigger breach blast radius**  
Centralized age-verification databases are catnip for attackers. If your workflow collects government IDs, selfies, or facial embeddings to “prove adulthood,” you have created a **high-value identity store**. A compromise means durable identity theft, not just password resets ([OWASP: Sensitive Data](https://owasp.org/www-project-top-ten/2017/A3_2017-Sensitive_Data_Exposure); [FTC: Retain only what you need](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/protecting-personal-information-guide-business-0)).

2) **Linkability and tracking**  
When an app store’s age category becomes the upstream truth and developers must verify against it, the resulting identifiers and consent tokens can be used to **correlate activity across apps** unless carefully designed with **privacy-preserving tokens** ([W3C Verifiable Credentials overview](https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-overview/); [VC Data Model 2.0](https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model-2.0/)).

3) **Fishing for phishers**  
“Upload your ID to continue” is a phisher’s dream. Any statewide push that normalizes ID submission online will **increase convincing phishing** and MFA-fatigue style attacks targeting parents and teens ([Cofense: ID & selfie phishing](https://cofense.com/blog/the-dangerous-blend-of-phishing-for-government-ids-and-facial-recognition-video/)).

4) **Shadow retention**  
Even when teams intend to “verify and delete,” ad-hoc logs, analytics events, debug screenshots, crash dumps, and vendor mirrors tend to **quietly retain artifacts**. That undermines data-minimization claims and expands incident-response scope ([OWASP Logging CS](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Logging_Cheat_Sheet.html); [OWASP ASVS §7.1](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OWASP/ASVS/v4.0.3/4.0/OWASP%20Application%20Security%20Verification%20Standard%204.0.3-en.pdf)).

5) **Vendor risk and legal coupling**  
SB 2420 intertwines app stores, age-assurance vendors, and developers. Each additional party brings **new attack surface, new SOC maturity assumptions, and new breach notification paths**, all under enforcement theories like deceptive trade practices ([bill analysis](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/analysis/html/SB02420H.HTM); [NIST Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework)).

## Practical guidance: how to comply without torching privacy

If you must support Texas users, design like an adversary is watching.

- **Prefer possession-based age proofs over data hoarding.** Use **on-device age tokens** or **selective-disclosure credentials** so your system learns only “over 18” or “parental consent present,” not the date of birth or ID number. Think W3C verifiable credentials or ISO mobile driver’s license style selective-disclosure, with **unlinkable, per-relying-party tokens** ([W3C VCs](https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-overview/); [Bellovin on limits](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/~smb/papers/age-verify.pdf); [New America brief](https://newamerica.org/oti/briefs/exploring-privacy-preserving-age-verification/)).  
- **Zero-retention by default.** If policy or vendor forces ID checks, **hash and immediately discard raw images**. Prove eligibility, **store only a short-lived, scoped token**, and keep a separate audit trail that shows “a compliant check happened” without storing PII ([FTC data minimization](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/protecting-personal-information-guide-business-0); [FPF data minimization paper](https://fpf.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/FPF_Data-Minimization.pdf)).  
- **Kill implicit logging.** Disable verbose logs during verification, scrub PII from exceptions, and put log scrubbing in CI. Make “no PII in logs” a test, not a wish ([OWASP Logging CS](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Logging_Cheat_Sheet.html); [ASVS controls](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OWASP/ASVS/v4.0.3/4.0/OWASP%20Application%20Security%20Verification%20Standard%204.0.3-en.pdf)).  
- **Vendor containment.** If you onboard an age-assurance provider, insist on: DPIA/TRA, **data-flow diagrams**, encryption posture, penetration-test reports, **data-deletion SLAs**, and incident-response integration ([NIST Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework)).  
- **Threat model the consent loop.** Assume attackers will impersonate parents. Require **out-of-band parental confirmation** and rate-limit consent flows ([Cofense example](https://cofense.com/blog/the-dangerous-blend-of-phishing-for-government-ids-and-facial-recognition-video/)).  
- **Transparency reports.** Publish aggregate stats on verification volumes, failures, and deletion latency. The sunlight disciplines your own retention and provides evidence of good-faith privacy engineering ([NIST Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework)).  
- **Plan for circumvention.** Users will VPN or sideload to dodge friction. Keep security controls proportional and avoid collecting more data than the attacker would surrender to a non-compliant alternative.

## My take as a security practitioner

The security goal is legitimate. The implementation vector is risky. By driving the market toward identity proofs for everyday app use, SB 2420 **increases the amount of sensitive data that must exist**, then asks a fragmented ecosystem to protect it indefinitely. That is not where you want your breach exposure.

Could we do age assurance with **privacy-preserving design**? Yes. But that requires strict data minimization, unlinkable tokens, verifiable deletion, and third-party audits. Most orgs are not there yet, and many vendors still monetize the very signals we need to minimize.

Bottom line: **be wary**. If you operate in Texas, implement the thinnest possible verification that satisfies the statute. Avoid collecting real IDs unless you have no alternative, and if you must, do it through a provider that supports **selective disclosure and immediate deletion**. Treat every new field you collect as a future data-breach headline, because that is how attackers will treat it.

## For policy folks tracking the bigger picture

SB 2420 lands in a legal environment that is increasingly comfortable with age checks, as the **[Supreme Court’s Texas pornography-site ruling](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/us-supreme-court-poised-rule-challenge-texas-age-check-online-porn-2025-06-27/)** showed this summer ([more coverage](https://apnews.com/article/12a73197796fe8c4bef0d888259543cf)). That momentum will encourage more states to follow. Meanwhile, trade associations are challenging SB 2420 on First Amendment and overbreadth grounds, and large platforms are **[warning about “real privacy and safety risks”](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=btkirlj8)** as they implement.

Security teams should plan for **multi-state divergence**, adopt **portable privacy architectures**, and assume further mandates on **parental dashboards**, **feature gating**, and **data-broker suppression** are coming. Build the privacy-preserving plumbing now so you are not forced into emergency identity collection later.
