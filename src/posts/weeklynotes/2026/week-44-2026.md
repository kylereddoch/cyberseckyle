## The vibe

Overall, pretty okay and chill. I had some bad neck pain toward the end of the week, which put a bit of a damper on things. Otherwise, I was having fun and feeling curious about where a few new ideas might go.

## Highlights

* &#x1F4F1; **I have been having fun with the iPhone Duo.** After the preorder mess in [last week's notes](/notes/2026/week-43-2026/), it has been nice to actually enjoy the phone. Trying apps, seeing what developers are doing with the extra space, and finding the little things that still need adjusting have all been part of it. My own website needed some of that attention, too. I tightened up the layouts for the Duo's shorter views and fixed the navigation so an open menu would not get stranded after resizing. Using the site on the phone made those rough edges much easier to spot.

* &#x1F4CA; **I shared Hinge Habits**, my little app for tracking iPhone Duo folds. It works with Stephen Robles' Count Duo Folds shortcut and adds daily counts, colorful widgets, and achievements, including The Foldfather. LOL. I also spent time working through Lock Screen widget ideas. There is something fun about taking a very specific new-phone habit and making a little app around it. The [Hinge Habits page](https://hinge-habits.cyberseckyle.chatgpt.site/) has more about it.

* &#x1F4D3; **I started another app project.** This one is a habit-and-journal app, and I am interested to see how it plays out. I have been exploring a more personal notebook feel, with artwork, themes, and a Today screen that feels like it belongs together. Getting that to work on both a regular iPhone and the Duo has been part of the process. It is still early, so I will have more to share as it takes shape.

* &#x1F3A8; **RelayByte and StylePort got some attention.** I redesigned the [RelayByte website](https://relaybyte.dev/) with a studio video and a clearer home for the apps I am building. [StylePort](https://styleport.app/) also reached a couple of milestones: the direct Mac release went up, and I submitted build 3 to Apple along with the optional tips. Submission is its own little milestone, even while the review process is still ahead of it.

* &#x1F5C2;&#xFE0F; **I kept working on how I show Trayage.** I updated the walkthrough on the [Trayage website](https://trayage.app/), wrote a guide to its smart filters, and refreshed the existing App Store featuring nomination. A lot of the work around an app happens outside the app itself: explaining what it does, showing the actual workflow, and making it easier for someone to decide whether it would help them.

* &#x1F50E; **There was client security work in the mix, too.** I reviewed an invoice PDF that endpoint protection had quarantined and compared the alert with a sandbox report for the same file. The reports did not tell the same story, so I spent time sorting out what belonged to the document and what appeared to be background Adobe activity. I kept the remaining questions open rather than treating either the detection or a clean sandbox result as the whole answer.

## Worth sharing

* **[Join me on Cackles!](https://cackles.club/invite?ref=2)** A friend built this, and it puts Clubhouse to shame! It is a live audio app for hanging out and talking with people, and it is optimized for the iPhone Duo, too. Definitely worth checking out. &#x1F399;&#xFE0F;
* **[A Real Microsoft Sign-in Page Can Still Be Phishing: What MSPs Should Know About MadApe](/blog/microsoft-365-device-code-phishing-madape-msp/)** looks at device-code phishing and the practical questions it raises for MSPs. This was a research-based article, with a focus on what to review in Microsoft 365 rather than assuming that a familiar sign-in page makes the whole interaction safe.
* **[Making My Website Fit the iPhone Duo](/blog/making-my-website-fit-the-iphone-duo/)** covers the layout work I mentioned above, with screenshots of the closed phone and the open screen in both orientations. It also gets into the difference between what I can change on my site and what Safari controls.
* **[How to Use Trayage's Smart Filters to Review Your Mac Downloads](https://trayage.app/blog/how-to-use-trayage-smart-filters/)** walks through presets, custom rules, and checking matching files before cleanup. Finding a match is the start of a review; it does not decide whether you still need the file.
* **[The Defender's Dispatch, Issue 012: A Warning Is Not a Stop](/newsletter/defenders-dispatch/issue-012/2026-10-31/)** went out on Saturday. The theme was checking what a security control actually did, rather than treating a warning or a reassuring control name as proof that something was stopped.
* On Mastodon, I boosted **[Stephen Robles' post about building apps for the Duo and Apple TV](https://mastodon.social/@stephenrobles/117518941542253884)**. More people making things for the Duo is very much my kind of distraction right now.
* On X, I reposted **[Paul Solt's post about the skills he uses to build apps](https://x.com/PaulSolt/status/2116705214531018810)**, including SwiftUI, concurrency, testing, and repeatable project workflows. Pretty relevant with all the app work I have been doing lately.

&#x270C;&#xFE0F; That is it for this week. I hope you have a great week and remember to be kind to each other!
