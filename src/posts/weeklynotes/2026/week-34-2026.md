## The vibe

Drained, proud, and ready for a cold front.

I did not feel like myself for much of the week, but I still had a few things I was genuinely happy about. I am hoping the sick feeling passes soon and Texas remembers that summer does eventually have to end.

## Highlights

* &#x1F6E0;&#xFE0F; I released [ReelArrange 0.3.0](/projects/reelarrange/) and [RipFoundry for Windows 1.3.1](/projects/ripfoundry-for-windows/) this week. ReelArrange now handles more of the weird episode naming I keep finding, remembers movie and TV source folders separately, and can stay open when I am ready to process another title. RipFoundry also got a fix for DVD extras so the names I review make it into Jellyfin instead of every extra inheriting the disc title.
* &#x1F331; [Retro Garden](/projects/retro-garden/) got a pretty big update too. Version 0.2.0 adds Pagefind search, responsive images, journal pagination, breadcrumbs, better IndieWeb and SEO markup, optional Tinylytics and webmentions, and more thorough checks on both Windows and Ubuntu. I wanted to modernize the publishing side without losing the handmade, personal-web feel that made me build the theme in the first place.
* &#x1F4FA; I am still loving my Jellyfin server and having my own media server. I know I have been talking about it a lot lately, but it has been nice building a collection that feels like mine and having little tools of my own to help manage it. LOL.
* &#x1F3EB; The first week of school went well. That also means we are back to early-morning tennis practice, so the alarms are once again going off earlier than I would like.
* &#x1F321;&#xFE0F; I am so done with this Texas heat. I used to think I wanted to be cremated when I died. After this summer, I have changed my mind. I have had enough experience with the heat already. LOL.
* &#x1F922; I was sick to my stomach and did not feel well for pretty much the whole week. Blah!
* &#x1F4BC; Work is same same...but different. That is probably the best way I can explain it right now.

## Worth sharing

* I wrote about why [a hotel Wi-Fi password does not make the network safe](/blog/that-hotel-wi-fi-password-does-not-make-the-network-safe/). A password tells you who may join the network; it does not tell you whether the gateway, DNS, or captive portal can be trusted.
* In [The Model Is Not the Security Boundary](/blog/the-model-is-not-the-security-boundary-how-to-secure-ai-workflows/), I looked at the identities, data, tools, approvals, logs, and failure paths around an AI workflow. Those are the controls that matter when a model gets something wrong or an attacker tries to manipulate it.
* I started the Light Offensive to Think Defensively track of my security how-to series with [building a safe lab with snapshots](/blog/cyberseckyle-security-how-to-series-light-offensive-to-think-defensively-part-1-build-a-safe-lab-with-snapshots/) and [running a defensive phishing simulation](/blog/cyberseckyle-security-how-to-series-light-offensive-to-think-defensively-part-2-phishing-simulation-to-study-prompts-and-controls/).
