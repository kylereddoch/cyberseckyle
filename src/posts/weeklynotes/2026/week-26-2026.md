## The vibe

This week still felt heavy in a lot of ways. I was not really feeling like myself for part of it, and the stress and anxiety from everything going on has definitely been hanging around. At the same time, I kept trying to move things forward where I could: writing, site cleanup, the how-to series, and a few small quality-of-life fixes around the site.

Sometimes that is what a week looks like. Not amazing. Not terrible. Just trying to keep going.

## Highlights

* &#x1F9E0; I shared a little on Mastodon about not feeling like myself lately. I have been more withdrawn, less interested in the things I usually enjoy, and just not in the best frame of mind. I am trying to give myself some grace, but it has been a tough stretch.
* &#x1F6E1;&#xFE0F; I kept the **CybersecKyle Security How-To Series** moving. This included roadmap and scheduling work, preview image work, and publishing two more Home Network and Devices posts: one on safer file storage with encryption and snapshots, and another on IoT quarantine and DNS choices.
* &#x1F319; I did some dark mode and readability polish on the site after a follower pointed out an issue. It was a good reminder that the little details matter, especially when people are actually reading the site in different themes and contexts. It ended up being a small issue with Javascript.
* &#x1F510; I added a dedicated [Privacy](/privacy/) page and linked it from the footer and site directory. It covers the usual site privacy details, Tinylytics, guestbook messages, Webmentions, and gives me a reusable privacy-policy URL for future apps or projects.
* &#x1F4DD; I wrote about the gatekeeping problem in IT and cybersecurity hiring. That one came from thinking about how often the industry says it wants learners and problem solvers, then filters people out with very specific tool names, titles, and experience requirements.
* &#x2696;&#xFE0F; I also wrote about AI security liability and the uncomfortable reality that if an AI tool makes the wrong security decision, the company still has to own the outcome.
* &#x1F5A5;&#xFE0F; I am still poking around the macOS 27 beta, and the [default menu bar spacing](https://infosec.exchange/@cyberseckyle/116829850636671173) has been throwing me off. It is a tiny thing, but apparently tiny UI spacing issues are exactly the kind of thing my brain likes to latch onto. LOL.
