## The vibe

Proud, relieved, and already thinking about what comes next.

After the move, the internet mess, and the general chaos of the past couple of weeks, this week gave me a pretty big personal milestone: I released my first app.

That still feels a little strange to write. I have built plenty of tools and side projects over the years, but putting a native Mac app out into the world under my own name feels different. There is a lot more involved than getting the code to work, and I learned quite a bit about that part this week too.

## Highlights

* &#x1F680; I officially released [Trayage](https://trayage.app/), my first native macOS app. Trayage helps you review the installers, likely duplicate downloads, older files, large files, and recent downloads that have collected in your Downloads folder. It does not automatically delete anything. You choose what to clean up, confirm the action, and Trayage moves those files to the system Trash.
* &#x1F9EA; Shipping the app was only part of the work. I spent a lot of time testing the direct download, purchase and license flow, update process, support pages, privacy details, and all the other pieces people should be able to trust when they buy an app. It is not the flashy side of a launch, but it matters just as much as the app itself.
* &#x1F3F7;&#xFE0F; I also introduced [RelayByte](https://relaybyte.dev/), the brand I will use for the independent apps I build. RelayByte gives the apps a shared home while still letting each one have its own identity. It is the brand behind the apps, but when you find them in the Mac App Store, the developer and seller name will still be **Kyle Reddoch**.
* &#x1F3A8; Trayage is the first RelayByte app, and [StylePort](https://styleport.app/) is already in development and coming soon. StylePort is a privacy-first UserCSS manager built around giving Safari users more control over how the websites they visit look. There is still work to do, but I am excited about where it is going.
* &#x1F4BB; The day-job side of things is still work. LOL. I am still working from home, though, and I am definitely not complaining about that.
* &#x1F34E; The Apple event took over a decent part of my Mastodon feed this week. I spent the event bouncing between the iPhone 18 Pro and the iPhone Duo, joking about how much money Apple was about to take from me, and reacting to everything else along the way. My wife's 18 Pro is secured. Now I am waiting for the Duo preorder for me.
