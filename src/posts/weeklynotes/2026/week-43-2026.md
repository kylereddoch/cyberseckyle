Late again. Apparently writing weekly notes on time is still a work in progress. LOL.

## The vibe

Excited, frustrated, and finally relieved. I was really looking forward to the Duo, and the runaround took a lot of the fun out of it. By the weekend, I was ready to be done with the whole ordeal and just enjoy the phone. It felt good to end the week excited about it again.

## Highlights

* &#x1F4F1; **The Best Buy preorder turned into a mess.** I had already cancelled my original AT&T preorder after finding launch-day pickup through Best Buy. On Thursday, I called the store and was told my phone was there waiting for Friday. I was excited enough to ask whether I could collect it early. By Friday morning, I was at the coffee shop, running on very little sleep and waiting for Best Buy to open. Then I learned that the store had received two Duos, both 256GB. My order was for a 512GB Night Sky model. The day ended with a delayed order, no availability date, and a warning that it could be cancelled on November 6. I wrote the [full preorder and launch-day story](/blog/iphone-duo-best-buy-preorder-launch-day/) on Saturday. That Thursday reassurance did not age well.

* &#x1F64C; **I finally got an iPhone Duo on Sunday, October 25.** I went back to looking at local AT&T stock and asked what was actually available. At one store, I was told the three Star White 256GB Duos were being held for new lines rather than upgrades. I [posted about that frustration](https://x.com/thecyberseckyle/status/2114396162564194315), too. To get one, I had to **add a new line to my account**. My plan is to transfer the phone to my existing line and then cancel the new one. Having to add a line just to buy a phone they had in stock was ridiculous. By lunchtime, I could finally [post that I had a Duo](https://x.com/thecyberseckyle/status/2114404318917042405). I got it through AT&T and settled for 256GB for now, with the intention of moving to 512GB when stock improves. It was not the configuration I had wanted, but after that week, actually having the phone felt like a win.

* &#x1F50E; **Client security work sent me into browser download history.** The same suspicious installer had appeared in two unrelated client environments. Antivirus quarantined it, but I still wanted to understand how it arrived. I worked on a PowerShell collector and caught a problem in my own collection logic: it was skipping browser databases that needed a closer look. After revising it, I found a matching download record. That gave me useful context without proving execution or the complete delivery route. I turned the investigation into an anonymized case study because the collection mistake and the limits of the result were worth sharing with other MSPs and defenders.

* &#x1F6E0;&#xFE0F; **A QuickBooks backup problem took some digging.** The company file passed Verify Data, but the backup still failed. Looking through `QBWin.log` pointed to an excessively long temporary path under nested search-index folders. Testing with a copy of the company data in a shorter location produced a successful backup to the thumb drive. One of those support jobs where the useful clue was buried in a log, and the test mattered more than another guess about the destination drive.

* &#x1F4BB; **I kept working on DMGator**, my Mac app project for installing apps from disk images and cleaning up afterward. This week included clearer installation screens, more useful error messages, and testing what happens with updates, damaged images, and images that do not contain an installable app. The backup and restore side got attention as well. It is still development work, but I like seeing the awkward cases get handled more clearly instead of only polishing the successful path.

* &#x1F680; **There was some Trayage housekeeping, too.** I worked on its PeerPush listing and added the PeerPush badge to the [Trayage website](https://trayage.app/). Smaller bits of launch work, but they are still part of getting an app in front of people. Back here, I also fixed the site's X large-image card setup after seeing how poorly the iPhone article preview looked when shared.
