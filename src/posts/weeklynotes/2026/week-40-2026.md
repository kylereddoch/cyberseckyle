## The vibe

Pretty busy, with a lot of my attention wrapped up in Trayage.

Between the launch and trying to get it into Apple's review process, it felt like there was always something else to get ready or follow up on. I got quite a bit done this week, but there was plenty still on my mind when I moved on to the next thing.

## Highlights

* &#x1F680; [Trayage launched on Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/trayage) on Friday, October 2. Getting the listing ready, putting together the images, and talking about the app took up a fair amount of the week. I also made a short, silent promo video and [shared it on X](https://x.com/thecyberseckyle/status/2106419897391808912). Getting people to actually see the app has been slow going, so I have been asking for feedback on both Trayage and how I explain it.
* &#x1F34E; The Mac App Store side of Trayage was still a waiting game at the end of the week. I was trying to get it moving through Apple's review process while the direct download was already available. I also [opened up beta testing](https://testflight.apple.com/join/BtqzAZem) for people who want to try the next round of improvements.
* &#x1F30A; I released [Drift](/projects/drift/), my free, open-source Chrome extension for sharing to Mastodon. Version 1.0.0 was approved for the Chrome Web Store on September 30. It started with something I wanted for myself: highlight a passage, click the extension, and have the quote, title, and link ready to edit. I also gave it a home at [drift.kylereddoch.me](https://drift.kylereddoch.me/). It is nice to have this one available for other people to use now.
* &#x1F3A8; Apparently the Catppuccin themes for Xcode last week were not enough. LOL. This week I put together [Catppuccin for Windows Terminal](https://github.com/kylereddoch/catppuccin-windows-terminal), with all four flavors: Latte, Frappé, Macchiato, and Mocha. The colors come from the official Catppuccin port; my project packages them with an installer so people can add the schemes without having their existing Terminal settings rewritten.
* &#x1F9ED; [StylePort](https://styleport.app/) got some attention too. I worked on the website's search metadata and social previews, put together a new promotional image, and started planning how to handle a launch waitlist. There is still work to do before the Safari app is ready, but I am getting the pieces around it ready as well.
* &#x1F3E0; I kept working on the household app I have mentioned in the past couple of notes. The idea is still to keep the practical details of running a home somewhere useful: instructions, photos, manuals, reminders, and the things someone else would need to know while you are away. I spent more time working through the scope across iPhone, iPad, and Mac. It is still in development.
* &#x1F4EC; I did more work behind the scenes on [The Defender's Dispatch](/newsletter/). Community Signal submissions now have a private review queue, with the research and source links alongside each item so I can decide what belongs in an issue. I also added optional Facebook Page sharing to the blog's publishing workflow, which saves me another manual step when I want an article to go there.
* &#x1F4F0; I started working on a review of Enzo Reader, which I have been trying for catching up on RSS inside ChatGPT. I have an opening draft and research notes, but I want more time with it before finishing the review. Getting a readable digest is useful; I also want to check how well it represents the articles it is summarizing.
* &#x1F91D; I joined [Buildside](https://www.buildside.app/) and spent more time connecting with other people building their own apps and projects. I also added a small MakerMap badge to this site and Drift's website. Between all the launch work, it has been good to make some room for seeing what other people are working on too.
* &#x1F4F1; And yes, I am still counting down to the iPhone Duo preorder. I [posted about having thirteen days left](https://x.com/thecyberseckyle/status/2106420643185201584) on Saturday. Apparently I can keep several app projects going and still find time to watch that date get closer. I did step away for some family time Friday evening, though!

## Worth sharing

* I wrote [Microsoft 365 Tenant Migrations Need an MSP Plan Beyond Email](/blog/microsoft-365-tenant-migrations-need-an-msp-plan-beyond-email/) about the work that can disappear behind a successful mailbox transfer report. Domain changes, staff sign-ins, shared access, recovery, and the support people need afterward all belong in the plan.
* [Eavesdropping Does Not Require Breaking Encryption](/blog/eavesdropping-does-not-require-breaking-encryption/) looks at linked devices, compromised endpoints, and the other places private conversations can end up. An extra device receiving the messages can be enough; the encryption may be doing exactly what it was designed to do.
* In [Shadow AI Puts MSP Client Data Boundaries to the Test](/blog/shadow-ai-puts-msp-client-data-boundaries-to-the-test/), I wrote about the client information inside an ordinary support ticket. An MSP approving an AI tool for its own use does not settle whether every client's tickets, screenshots, and logs may be sent to it.
* [Security Signal Weekly: September 26–October 2, 2026](/blog/security-signal-weekly-september-26-october-2-2026/) covers ten stories involving access gateways, management systems, remote support, exposed credentials, and other things defenders need to keep an eye on. Several call for checking what happened before a patch was installed.
* On Mastodon, I [shared BleepingComputer's report about more than 543,000 valid credentials exposed in public GitHub repositories](https://infosec.exchange/@cyberseckyle/117366856739678658). The [report](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/over-543-000-valid-credentials-exposed-in-public-github-repositories/) covers Truffle Security's research into secrets that were still usable long after being exposed. Removing a key from a file and revoking that key are two separate jobs.
* I also boosted [Dark Web Informer's post about OSGINT](https://infosec.exchange/@DarkWebInformer/117350585169480160), an open-source tool for finding public information associated with a GitHub username or email address. The [repository](https://github.com/hippiiee/osgint) describes what it can retrieve, including email addresses associated with commits. It is one to look at when reviewing what your public GitHub activity reveals.
* On X, I shared [CM Alliance's September cyberattack and data-breach roundup](https://x.com/thecyberseckyle/status/2105707623068668223). The [full roundup](https://www.cm-alliance.com/cybersecurity-blog/major-cyber-attacks-data-breaches-ransomware-attacks-september-2026) brings the month's incidents, vulnerabilities, and advisories together, with links to the reporting. Useful if September's news got away from you while you were busy with everything else.
