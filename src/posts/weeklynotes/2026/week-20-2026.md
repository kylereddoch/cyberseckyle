## Worth sharing

* AI-generated fraud and identity spoofing are getting harder to treat as “normal” account fraud. This CyberScoop piece on [weaponized AI and identity spoofing](https://cyberscoop.com/ai-generated-fraud-identity-spoofing-defense-strategy/) is sponsored, but it still makes a useful point: static identity checks are going to struggle when attackers can scale synthetic identities, deepfake impersonation, and document fraud faster than most organizations can update their controls.
* Agentic AI is becoming one of those security problems that looks small until you realize how much access these tools can touch. [Why Agentic AI Is Security’s Next Blind Spot](https://thehackernews.com/2026/05/why-agentic-ai-is-securitys-next-blind.html) is worth reading because it frames AI agents less like “chatbots” and more like systems that can read, act, connect to tools, and create new lateral movement paths if nobody is reviewing permissions, inputs, and workflows.
* A cyber incident response plan should not be a 100-page binder nobody reads during an actual crisis. This article on the [5 key components of a cyber incident response plan](https://www.cm-alliance.com/cybersecurity-blog/5-key-components-of-a-cyber-incident-response-plan) is a good reminder that response plans need to be crisp, business-specific, scenario-driven, threat-informed, and tested. For MSPs, that means client-specific playbooks for ransomware, BEC, cloud compromise, and vendor/supply-chain events.
