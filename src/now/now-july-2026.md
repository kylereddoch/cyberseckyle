It has been a bit since I updated this page, and quite a lot has changed since January. Some of it has been exciting, some of it has been hard, and most of it has happened while I was just trying to keep the normal parts of life moving.

Here is the honest version of what I am focused on right now.

## Work

I am still deep in MSP life, where cybersecurity and IT support have a way of turning into people problems just as often as technical ones. The work is still a mix of security, troubleshooting, client support, vendor tools, and trying to make all the moving pieces work together without creating more friction for the people using them.

Lately I have been thinking a lot about trust and operational debt. A shiny new security or AI tool does not fix a messy process underneath it, and a technically correct answer is not very useful if the person asking for help no longer trusts you. That has been showing up in both my day-to-day work and the things I have been writing here.

## I finally finished my degree

This one deserves its own section: I officially graduated on April 30 with my **Bachelor of Science in Information Technology**.

That road took longer and changed shape more than I expected when I started it, but I finished. I am proud of myself for sticking with it, and it still feels a little strange not having another assignment waiting for me after work. For now, I am enjoying having that time and brain space back.

## Writing and sharing

I have been writing quite a bit. [Security Signal Weekly](/tags/security-signal-weekly/) is now my Friday look at the ten security stories I think matter most to defenders, IT teams, and MSPs. I am trying to make it more useful than a pile of headlines by explaining the operational impact and what someone should actually do next.

The [CybersecKyle Security How-To Series](/tags/cyberseckyle-howto-series/) is moving along too. I finished the Home Network and Devices section and have started working through the Power User and Small Team topics. The goal is still practical security that normal people can actually use without turning their home or small business into a miniature enterprise SOC.

WWDC also sent me down a pretty deep Apple Intelligence and Siri AI security rabbit hole. That turned into writing about prompt injection, personal context, app actions, cloud trust, business policy, and who owns the failure when AI makes a security decision. More recently, I have been thinking and writing about privacy, including what the latest Waze terms mean for location data.

## Building things

I have started working on a couple of native Apple app ideas for iPhone, iPad, and Mac. I am building them with SwiftUI and learning as I go. I am not ready to share much about them yet, and I have not had as much time for them lately as I would like, but they are still very much in motion.

I also released [EmojiCodec](/projects/emojicodec/), a small VS Code extension that converts emoji between native characters, HTML entities, Unicode escapes, hexadecimal values, and Markdown shortcodes. It started as a tool I wanted for myself, which is usually how my favorite little projects begin.

This site continues to be a project of its own. I have been improving the publishing automation, polishing the RSS feed and dark mode, adding pages for [privacy](/privacy/) and [how I use AI](/ai/), and building a new [/watching page](/watching/) powered by my Trakt history. I still enjoy finding small ways to make the site more useful and more like my own corner of the web.

## Life lately

Life has been heavier than usual. My wife recently lost the job she had for 16 years, which immediately turned into a scramble around insurance and all the other practical things that come with a change like that. I have also had stretches where I have felt more withdrawn and not quite like myself. I am trying to be honest about that without making every difficult week into a dramatic life update.

My back and neck issues still shape how much I can do in a day, so I am working on giving myself a little more grace when my energy does not match my plans. Consistency matters, but so does knowing when to slow down.

There have been plenty of good family moments mixed in too. My oldest graduated from Texas Tech, my youngest made the tennis team again, and we have made time for movies, friends, and the normal small things that help a hard stretch feel a little lighter.

## What I am making room for right now

- Keeping the security writing useful without turning the site into a content machine.
- Moving the Apple app projects forward when I have the time and energy to do them well.
- Staying curious at work and focusing on the people and processes behind the tools.
- Spending time with family and friends instead of treating every free hour like it needs to become a project.
- Watching the World Cup and Formula 1, tinkering with an old ThinkPad running Kali Purple, and enjoying some things just because they are fun.

That is where things are right now: degree finished, plenty of ideas in motion, life a little messy, and me trying to keep moving without pretending I have to do all of it at once.
