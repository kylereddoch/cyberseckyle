It has been a while since I updated this page, and quite a bit has happened since the last one. I am still writing about security and working in the MSP world. Outside of that, I have spent a lot of time on self-hosting, physical media, and building tools that solve problems I keep running into myself.

Here is where things stand right now.

## Work

Work is same same...but different. That is still the best way I can explain it.

I am working from home and spending my days in the usual mix of cybersecurity, IT support, vendor tools, client problems, and all the strange issues that come with MSP life. The technical part matters, but I keep coming back to the operational side of security: whether a control works in the real world, whether people understand what is changing, and whether we can respond quickly enough when the time between a vulnerability disclosure and active exploitation keeps shrinking.

There is always another tool promising to fix everything. I am much more interested in whether the process underneath it is solid and whether it actually helps the people who have to use it.

## Graduation finally feels real

I officially finished my Bachelor of Science in Information Technology in April, but I finally got to walk the stage during a family trip to San Antonio in July. That made the whole thing feel more complete than simply seeing the degree show up in the mail.

We turned the trip into a family vacation, packed in plenty of sightseeing, ate some good food, and came home completely worn out. I also finally hung the degree in my home office. After the long and sometimes messy road it took to earn it, seeing it on the wall still makes me proud.

## Building around Jellyfin

My Jellyfin server has become one of my favorite projects. I have been ripping more of my physical media, finding things on the Internet Archive, learning how Jellyfin wants a library organized, and slowly building a collection that feels like mine instead of something that can disappear when a streaming service changes its mind.

That process also led to two new open-source projects.

[ReelArrange](/projects/reelarrange/) is a Windows app for matching downloaded movies and shows with TMDB, previewing a Jellyfin-friendly layout, and moving or copying everything into place. [RipFoundry for Windows](/projects/ripfoundry-for-windows/) guides the DVD side of the process, from MakeMKV through optional processing, validation, and the final transfer into the library. I also brought [RipFoundry back to Linux](/projects/ripfoundry-for-linux/) for people who want that workflow there.

All of them started with a problem I was tired of solving by hand. I have already found plenty of weird edge cases while using them, but honestly, working through those has been part of the fun. I like building something, putting it into my own routine, and then improving it based on what actually breaks.

I also released a fairly big update to [Retro Garden](/projects/retro-garden/). It has better search, images, navigation, IndieWeb support, and publishing checks now, while still keeping the handmade personal-web feeling I wanted from it in the first place.

## Writing and The Defender's Dispatch

I launched [The Defender's Dispatch](/newsletter/), my new weekly cybersecurity newsletter. Starting it involved much more than writing an email. I built the signup and confirmation flow, worked through the design and sending process, and added an online archive so each issue also has a home on the site.

The newsletter gives me a different place to write than [Security Signal Weekly](/tags/security-signal-weekly/). Security Signal is still my rundown of the stories defenders, IT teams, and MSPs should have on their radar. The Defender's Dispatch is more personal and gives me room to connect one important story, a practical action, something useful to read, and a few things from around the site.

The [CybersecKyle Security How-To Series](/tags/cyberseckyle-howto-series/) is still moving too. I finished the Blue Team Fundamentals section and moved into light offensive work that helps people think more defensively. The focus is still on safe labs, clear boundaries, and exercises that teach something useful without pretending everyone needs a full enterprise security team at home.

## Site work

This site is never really finished, and I am okay with that. Lately I have been improving social sharing, making the newsletter archive feel like a real part of the site, and continuing to clean up the smaller things I notice while publishing.

The [/watching page](/watching/) has also been a source of frustration. Changes around Trakt authentication left it stuck with old data, so I have been untangling that instead of enjoying the simple little page it was supposed to be. Sometimes owning your corner of the web means you also own every weird API change that breaks it.

## Life lately

School is back in session, which means our house is back to early alarms and morning tennis practice. We did the usual back-to-school shopping, and I am settling into the family routine again after a busy summer.

I have also felt worn down and sick more than I would like lately. The Texas heat has not helped, and my back and neck still have a vote in how much I get done on any given day. I am proud of what I have shipped, but I am trying not to turn that pride into pressure to keep producing at the same pace every week.

The native Apple app ideas I mentioned in July have not disappeared. They just are not at the front of the line right now. I would rather be honest about where my attention is than pretend every idea is moving forward at once.

## What I am making room for right now

- Enjoying my Jellyfin library instead of always finding another thing to fix in it.
- Improving ReelArrange and RipFoundry based on real use without rushing every idea into a release.
- Keeping The Defender's Dispatch and Security Signal useful and sustainable.
- Writing practical security guidance that respects people's time and the environments they actually have.
- Spending time with my family and letting rest count as something worthwhile.
- Hoping Texas remembers that summer eventually has to end.

That is where I am right now: proud of what I finished, excited about what I am building, a little tired, and trying to leave enough room to actually enjoy it.
