My iPhone Duo Preorder and the Best Buy Launch-Day Shit Show

I left my AT&T preorder for Best Buy's launch-day pickup promise. A store confirmation, two phones, a delayed order, and a cancellation warning later, here we are.

I started iPhone Duo launch day running on too little sleep, too much excitement, and coffee. I ended it with no Duo, an order with no pickup date, and an email telling me Best Buy might cancel the whole thing on November 6.

Somewhere in between, “your phone is here waiting for you” became “we don’t know when your item will be available.”

So, yeah. This preorder and launch-day experience has been a shit show.

If you followed me on X yesterday, October 23, you watched my mood change in real time. I wanted to put the story in one place, because the frustrating part goes well beyond having to wait for a phone.

AT&T was where this started

I originally tried to preorder through AT&T. Their site kept crashing, and by the time I could get an order through, I was looking at an end-of-November delivery date.

Then someone on X tipped me off that Best Buy still had release-day pickup available. That was the entire appeal. I could stop fighting the carrier’s website and get the phone on launch day.

On October 16, I posted that I had cancelled the AT&T order and switched to Best Buy. I even called the heads-up a lifesaver. At the time, it felt like I had managed to rescue preorder day.

My order was for the 512GB iPhone Duo in Night Sky, on AT&T. I had made a purchasing decision based on the pickup date Best Buy gave me. I gave up the other order because this one was supposed to arrive sooner.

That decision looks a lot different now.

“At least I know I am getting it on release day”

On Thursday, October 22, I called my local Best Buy to check on the order. I came away from that call being told my phone was at the store, waiting for Friday’s launch.

I was excited enough to ask whether I could just pick it up that day. No luck. LOL.

Here is what I posted afterward:

But at least I know I am getting it on release day! The streak continues…

That sentence aged beautifully, obviously.

By Friday morning, the website was showing “getting ready.” I posted that I would have been worried if I hadn’t called the store the day before. That call was the reason I still felt confident. I thought I had checked the thing that mattered: whether my phone was actually there.

I hadn’t slept well because I was excited. I was at my local coffee shop, drinking coffee and waiting for Best Buy to open. I was already talking about the apps I wanted to try. In a quote post about Wavy Road, I said I couldn’t wait to play it on my Duo.

This was supposed to be the fun part.

Two phones. Neither was my order.

Then came the launch-day reality: my preorder was not ready for me.

As I reported on X that morning, my store had only received two Duos, both 256GB. Two. My order was for 512GB.

That was what I was told about my local store, not a nationwide inventory count. But it was enough to make Thursday’s reassurance fall apart. How had I been told my order was there if the phones the store received weren’t even the capacity I had ordered?

By early afternoon, I was posting about the launch-day promise, the store’s assurance, and customer support doing nothing to make it right. I was pissed off. My posts were not subtle about it.

I can understand a popular device selling out. I can understand a shipment arriving late. What I cannot understand is being told my specific order is waiting for me, planning around that information, and then finding out it isn’t.

If the person I spoke with was looking at an estimate rather than a physical phone, that distinction needed to be made clear. If the system showed something the store couldn’t actually fulfill, somebody needed to explain that. From my side of the counter, I had been given a reason to expect a phone and then left to chase answers when it didn’t happen.

The evening update somehow made it worse

At 6:16 p.m. Central, I posted a screenshot of Best Buy’s delay email. It said they would work to have the item ready in the next few days. It also said that if it wasn’t available by November 6, they would cancel the order and notify me by email.

My reaction was pretty straightforward:

Yeah, @BestBuy you better NOT cancel my order!

I had already given up one preorder to place this one. Now I was facing the possibility of losing this order too, because the retailer couldn’t supply the phone it had let me order for launch-day pickup.

At 7:41 p.m., I shared the updated order page, the screenshot at the top of this post. It finally showed the preorder as delayed and said Best Buy didn’t know when the item would be available.

So the email was talking about the next few days, the order page had no date, and November 6 was sitting there as a cancellation deadline. None of that gave me a dependable answer to the question I actually had: when am I getting my phone?

By 9:07 p.m., I had signed off for the night:

Good night X! Going to bed Duo-less. 😭😩

A fairly accurate review of the day’s results.

My timeline had two very different launch days

All day, X was full of people getting their Duos, showing off apps, and enjoying the launch. I was sharing some of that excitement too. I reposted Chan Karunamuni’s look at the opening and closing transition and Adithya’s collection of apps made for the Duo. Those were the things I wanted to be exploring myself.

Instead, I was also reposting Quinn Nelson’s account of his Best Buy and Verizon experience. He said his number had ported while he was still waiting for the phone from Best Buy, leaving him without service. He described asking Verizon to activate his existing phone, being told they couldn’t, and then waiting on Best Buy’s hold music with the Verizon representative.

That is his account, not something that happened to my line. But reading it while dealing with my own preorder mess made the whole launch feel even more ridiculous. A delayed gadget is frustrating enough. A carrier change that leaves someone without working service adds a much bigger problem.

I also quote-posted Vadim Yuryev’s launch-video post with a frustrated comment about people being unable to get their phones. That was me venting. A creator’s post doesn’t establish why my store was short of inventory, and I don’t have evidence connecting those devices to my missing order.

Other people replied to my delay update saying they were dealing with similar problems. That didn’t fix anything, but it did make clear that I wasn’t the only person trying to reconcile a promised pickup with an order that had gone nowhere.

The explanation relayed to me, and what I cannot verify

After all of this, a personal contact passed along an account attributed to someone in Best Buy’s corporate operation. I did not speak with that person myself, and I have no internal documents to verify what was relayed.

An unverified account, not an official explanation

The information below came through an indirect personal contact. It is not a statement from Best Buy or Apple, and it does not establish that Apple deliberately withheld stock. My own order history and screenshots are separate from these claims.

The account described a substantial gap between the inventory Best Buy expected from Apple and what it actually received. According to that explanation, preorder dates had been based on expected allocations, and the shortfall left stores unable to meet them.

I am deliberately not putting a percentage on that claim. The figures in the account were inconsistent, so there isn’t a reliable number to repeat.

The same account claimed there was no inventory in Best Buy’s warehouses at the time and no incoming inventory showing, while some stores had received a small number of devices. It also described uneven distribution between regions, no dependable restock date, and concern that missed launch-day orders could push later preorders back as well. Claims about internal reviews of the situation were part of that account too. I cannot independently confirm any of it.

If an allocation shortfall is what happened, that would help explain the shortage. It would still leave my biggest question unanswered: why was I told my phone was already at the store?

The contact also gave me their own opinion that Apple’s estimates were a better guide than an earlier date displayed by a retailer or carrier. They mentioned January 2027 estimates for new Apple orders and worried that dates elsewhere, even in December, might slip. That is their assessment, not a verified forecast. I can’t turn an Apple estimate into a delivery date for my Best Buy order, and I am not going to tell anyone that every carrier’s date is false.

At this point, another confident prediction is the last thing I need. I want a date backed by a phone somebody can actually hand me.

What about people getting one without a preorder?

One of the things that frustrated me most was hearing accounts of people walking into Best Buy and getting a Duo without a preorder while other customers were still waiting.

I don’t have the order records behind those stories. I can’t say whether a particular device matched an outstanding preorder’s model, carrier, capacity, or pickup location. But the question is reasonable: when a matching device becomes available, shouldn’t an existing preorder get priority?

The explanation relayed to me was that some of those devices might have come from cancelled orders. That is a possible explanation, not a confirmed account of any specific sale.

Even if that is what happened, I still want to know why a cancelled preorder wouldn’t first go toward another compatible order already waiting. Best Buy could clear up a lot of anger by explaining how that queue works instead of leaving customers to piece it together from social posts and private conversations.

Now I have a cancellation deadline to worry about

The one potentially useful suggestion in the private account was to contact customer service as the November 6 cancellation deadline approaches and ask whether it can be extended.

I have not confirmed that Best Buy will do that for my order. I would want the extension and any new deadline confirmed in writing before relying on it. The November 6 date is from my email; someone else’s order may have a different date.

There was also speculation that people cancelling their orders could free up inventory. Maybe it will. That still doesn’t give me a pickup date.

This morning, October 24, I reposted Joe Rossignol saying Apple had told him stores would receive Duo deliveries daily. He explicitly said he didn’t know whether that meant every store, every day. That is worth keeping in mind, but it isn’t a promise about Best Buy inventory or my preorder.

I am tired of having to translate every update into a list of things it might mean.

I still want the phone. I wanted a better experience getting it.

Yes, I know it is a phone. I still have things to do, work to get through, and a life outside of an order-status page. I posted about having to get back to work yesterday even while I was annoyed and nursing a headache.

But I was excited about this. There is nothing wrong with looking forward to something, especially when you have made the effort to preorder it and checked that it will be there.

What spoiled the experience was the sequence: AT&T’s preorder problems, switching to Best Buy for an earlier date, being told the phone was in the store, finding out it wasn’t, and finally getting an email that introduced the possibility of cancellation. Every step gave me another reason to question the previous one.

Best Buy owes customers a clear account of what happened, realistic information about outstanding orders, and a way to keep those orders from disappearing while its own inventory problem remains unresolved. I also want an explanation for the confirmation I was given. “High demand” does not answer that.

By this morning, I was back on X admitting that the FOMO was hitting hard and asking how everyone liked their Duos. I still want to try the apps. I still want the phone. The excitement is there, underneath all the frustration.

I just wish launch day had ended with me setting it up instead of writing “Duo-less” before bed.
