GO Club Review: Why I Keep Tracking Steps and Water Together

My review of GO Club Plus on the iPhone Duo, including steps, water logging, widgets, Apple Watch, walking plans, pricing, privacy, and where it fits beside other apps.

I have been using GO Club for two things I already paid attention to: my steps and how much water I drink. Apple Health records steps, and I have used apps dedicated to either activity or hydration. GO Club stays on my iPhone Duo because it puts both habits in one place. Its widgets keep the numbers visible, and I can log water from my Apple Watch without opening my phone.

That is a more convincing reason to keep an app than an attractive screenshot. GO Club has plenty of those, and Apple named it a 2025 Apple Watch App of the Year finalist. The day-to-day combination of steps, water, widgets, and a quick Watch action is what gives it a place on my devices.

My verdict

GO Club Plus works for me because checking steps and recording a drink feel like parts of the same routine. I can see both on my Home Screen, use the Watch for water, and open the full app when I want the week’s activity or hydration history. On the Duo, the wide layout makes that history visible beside today’s progress. The compact layout keeps the immediate numbers and controls in view.

There are reasons to keep other apps around. Apple Health remains the record behind the step data, Apple Fitness handles broader activity, Pedometer++ offers a deeper step-focused view, and Waterllama goes further into drinks. GO Club’s advantage for me is the pairing and how easily I can reach it. This review is based on my use of GO Club Plus for steps, water, widgets, and Watch logging, along with screenshots I took on October 26. The walking-plan discussion below is about the feature and what the screen shows; a plan card alone cannot establish how well its advice adapts over time.

Steps without digging through Health

Apple Health already counts steps from an iPhone and can gather activity from an Apple Watch and compatible apps. GO Club says it reads that motion data through Health. Its job is to make the count useful when I glance at it: today’s number and goal first, with distance, active energy, floors, and earlier days close by.

The wide Steps screenshot at the top shows how the Duo changes that view. A weekly totals column sits on the left. The daily graph, goal progress, and today’s count stay on the right. On the compact screen, the totals column drops away, but the chart and large current count remain. Both screenshots show the same 1,230 steps and 41% goal progress, taken about a minute apart.

That difference matters more than simply stretching the phone interface across a larger display. The wide screen gives me history while I check today; the compact screen keeps the check quick. The screenshots establish what each layout showed at that moment. They do not measure step accuracy or prove how smoothly the app changes size while the phone folds.

Water is a quick action, not another chart to study

Water tracking requires an action from me. GO Club cannot know when I have taken a drink, so the log control matters more than a detailed graph I might only open once a week. On my Apple Watch, I use GO Club to add water without getting out the phone. In the full iPhone view, the illustrated bottle, amount consumed, daily goal, and add control are all prominent.

The compact layout removes that separate history column. It still shows the bottle, today’s consumed amount, goal progress, and the control for adding water. The wide and compact captures both show 32 ounces consumed and 34% of my daily goal. I can see what changes with the screen size without mistaking two images for a long-term sync test.

GO Club’s privacy policy says the app can write supported entries such as water intake to Apple Health when permission is granted. That is useful if Health is where you keep the longer record. It is still worth checking which app wrote an entry before correcting a mistake, especially when you use both a phone and Watch.

The widgets and Watch are why it stays close

My Duo Home Screen has separate GO Club widgets for steps and water. They put both numbers where I already look, without requiring me to open the app to find today’s totals. The Apple Watch matters for the other half of the routine: I log water there. That combination is the best feature for me, and it explains why I use Plus even though I have other health apps installed.

Apple’s developer spotlight says the GO Club team revised its widgets and worked on a standalone Watch experience after user feedback. That account adds useful background to the product, but the reason I keep the app is less abstract: the information is visible, and I can record water from my wrist. Whether a widget updates promptly or a Watch entry reaches the phone every time is a separate reliability question from how good either screen looks.

Walking plans add another layer

GO Club’s Smart Walking Plan and Golden Hour feature go beyond showing a daily step goal. Apple’s App Store feature describes a multiday plan that considers activity, weather, and UV information when suggesting a time to walk. GO Club’s App Store listing places those tools among its premium features.

The screen shows where the idea could help: a target, a streak view, and a prompt to use location to find a walking window. It also shows the limit of what I can conclude from a screenshot. I would judge a plan by how it responds to missed days, bad weather, and changes in my schedule, not by whether the first target card looks polished. The company’s terms describe its plans as general wellness suggestions, not medical advice.

How it compares with the apps I already use

I have used Apple Health, Apple Fitness, Pedometer++, and Waterllama. Health is the underlying place for step and other health records. Fitness is built around activity rings, workouts, and trends, with steps available in its summary. I would still use those for their broader records and activity view. GO Club puts the particular steps-and-water routine I care about closer to the surface.

Pedometer++ goes deeper on steps, distance, badges, graphs, and widgets. Waterllama has a broader drink catalogue, challenges, and more detailed hydration features. If I wanted to go deep on either one alone, those are sensible choices. GO Club earns its place for the opposite reason: I do not have to treat movement and water as two unrelated app visits. The widgets and Watch logging keep the combined routine practical.

That is why I think GO Club is better for this job. It is a claim about fit, not a claim that its step charts beat Pedometer++ or its beverage tools beat Waterllama. I can keep the underlying Apple data and still prefer a different front door for my daily check.

Pricing and value

GO Club is free to download. In the US, Go Plus costs $3.99 a month or $19.99 a year.

Plus adds more widgets and customization, deeper statistics, unlimited history, enhanced goals, walking plans, and Golden Hour. The App Store lists steps, water logging, widgets, and Apple Watch support among the app’s general features. I’m using Plus, but seeing steps and water on my Duo widgets and logging a drink from my Watch are what keep me using GO Club. If those are the only things you need, try the free version first. Pay for Plus when you know you’ll use what it adds.

A privacy claim that needs context

One of GO Club’s current App Store images says “nothing is collected or shared.” The developer’s privacy policy makes a narrower statement: GO Club says it does not store collected health information on its servers. It also describes account and purchase information, Firebase Analytics and messaging, RevenueCat for subscriptions, and inputs that can leave the device to generate a walking plan. Generated plans may be saved, and plan-related endpoints receive step-completion and mood-derived progress values.

That is not evidence that GO Club misuses health data; I have not audited its network traffic. It does mean the absolute language in the marketing image does not capture the distinctions in the policy. The App Store privacy label also lists some information that may be linked to a user and other data collected without that link. Apple notes that it has not verified the developer’s disclosures. I would read the policy and choose Health, location, notification, and account permissions with that fuller picture in mind.

Who I would recommend it to

GO Club fits someone who wants steps and water together and will actually use the shortcuts around that pair: a Home Screen glance and a quick Watch log. That is why it stays on my Duo. Open it wide and I get history beside today’s number; close it and the current goal remains easy to find.

If you mainly want step analysis, Pedometer++ has a stronger claim on your attention. If you care about logging many kinds of drinks, Waterllama is the more specialized tool. Apple’s own apps remain useful for the underlying health record and broader activity. GO Club makes the most sense when those jobs can be simpler because the two habits you check most often live together.

I’m keeping GO Club for the combined steps-and-water experience I can reach from my phone and Watch. That is the part I can speak to from daily use. I would judge the walking plan after using it over time, and I would read the privacy policy rather than rely on the broad App Store artwork.
