Why I run on Proton

I work in MSP and cybersecurity. Threat models change week to week, but the fundamentals stay the same: encrypt data at rest and in transit, minimize metadata, keep providers on a need-to-know diet, and use open source with third-party audits. Proton checks those boxes under Swiss privacy law and publishes audits across the suite.

Right now, Proton is having their Black Friday Deals. This year, you can get up to 75% off on their products. Below is the quick-scan version of each product, how I use it, and the current deal.

Proton VPN - private network layer that travels well

How I use it daily: I keep Proton VPN on whenever I am on hotel, café, or client guest Wi-Fi. Secure Core multi-hop protects on hostile networks, NetShield cuts trackers and malware domains, and the clients are open source with independent audits. For travel, the Plus servers reliably unlock major streaming platforms during testing by third parties.

Why it matters: A VPN should not leak IP, DNS, or WebRTC, and it should avoid quirky app breakage. Proton’s focus on privacy features and audits is the differentiator, not gimmicks. TechRadar’s 2025 review calls out the privacy commitment and the breadth of the network, which mirrors my experience.

How it beats the competition: The Plus plan supports 10 devices, which covers a phone-tablet-laptop setup plus a travel streaming stick. Some competitors cap fewer devices on similar tiers.

Proton Mail - encrypted email that does not farm your life

How I use it daily: I route client-facing aliases through custom domains and use message expiration for sensitive threads. Tracker protection strips spy pixels and known tracking links out of inbound mail, which helps keep engagement data from flowing back to senders.

Why it matters: Big inbox providers monetize attention and metadata. Proton Mail applies end-to-end and zero-access encryption so content stays private, and it supports modern transport safeguards like MTA-STS and DANE for delivery security outside the Proton network.

How it beats the competition: If you want private mail and a clean security model without ad targeting, Proton’s design choices are the point. You can pair Mail with Pass and Drive in one vendor without handing over behavioral data. The best value route right now is the Proton Unlimited bundle at 50% off.

Claim Black Friday deal: Get Proton Mail

Proton Drive - encrypted storage without data mining

How I use it daily: I share reports and exports via password-protected links with expiration dates, and I can revoke a link in one click if a recipient list changes. That reduces the blast radius when a link is forwarded.

Why it matters: Drive encrypts files and key metadata on device before upload. That keeps content out of ad pipelines and reduces breach fallout because the provider cannot read your data.

How it beats the competition: Many mainstream clouds scan files for policy, safety, or commercial reasons. Proton’s end-to-end model avoids provider access, while still giving you modern sharing controls like passwords and expirations.

Claim Black Friday deal: Get Proton Drive

Proton Pass - zero-access password manager with clean UX

How I use it daily: I keep unique, long passwords, TOTP codes, and secure notes in Pass. It is open source and independently audited, which is non-negotiable for a secrets manager. Proton also added Secure Links so you can share an item with a time-limited link when needed.

Why it matters: A manager should have a zero-knowledge architecture, encrypted metadata where possible, and clean cross-device sync without ad tracking. Pass checks those boxes and benefits from the same privacy posture as the rest of Proton’s suite.

How it beats the competition: During Black Friday, Pass Plus has deep discounts on its own, and if you plan to use multiple Proton services, getting Pass through Unlimited at 50% off is the simpler and cheaper path.
