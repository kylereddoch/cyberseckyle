Why CybersecKyle Has a New Look

I finally launched a redesigned version of CybersecKyle built around a cleaner IndieWeb-focused template, better content organization, and a site experience that feels more like where this website is headed.

Published on April 6, 2026, 4:30pm by Kyle Reddoch • 5 min read
Permalink

If you are coming here to read this post, you probably notice that CybersecKyle looks a little different now.

Actually, a lot different.

I finally launched a redesign that has been bouncing around in my head for a while, and I wanted to write a proper post about why I changed it, what changed, and what I was trying to improve instead of just quietly swapping themes and moving on. A lot of the thinking behind it overlaps with something else I wrote recently about human websites and polish.

Why I wanted to redesign it

My old setup worked, but it had started to feel like a site I had outgrown. That is especially funny to say after all the work I put into moving the site to Eleventy + GitHub Pages, but getting onto a better stack was only part of the job.

That does not mean it was bad. It just means the shape of the site and the shape of my writing were no longer lining up as well as I wanted. Over the past year or so, this site has turned into more than just a blog. It has become:

  • a home for my longer cybersecurity and IT writing
  • a place for weekly notes and smaller updates
  • a testing ground for IndieWeb ideas
  • a living system that includes search, OG images, Mastodon sharing, webmentions, reading, listening, and more

At a certain point, the old design was doing more “containing” than “supporting.”

I wanted something that felt calmer, more intentional, and more connected to the way I actually use the site now.

Why I went with this design

The new redesign is based on the IndieWeb Starter Blog, which I liked immediately because it felt like a better fit for the kind of personal site I want CybersecKyle to be.

It has a few things I really liked from the start:

  • it feels personal without feeling messy
  • it gives smaller content types like notes room to exist naturally
  • it is easier to browse
  • it has a stronger IndieWeb vibe without becoming hard to use
  • it gives the site a little more warmth and personality

I am also just a sucker for seeing something on someone else’s website and immediately thinking, “man, that would look good on my website.”

That probably will never fully go away, and honestly, I do not think I want it to. Part of the fun of having a personal website is seeing good ideas out in the wild and wanting to bring some of that inspiration home with you. What made this redesign feel worth doing, though, was that it was not only about the lipstick. This design also brought in a lot of backend structure and content organization that I had been wanting for a while. In that sense, it feels like a better foundation for the future too, even if I change the visual styling again later.

That balance mattered to me.

I did not want a site that felt like a product page. I also did not want a site that felt so stripped down that everything blurred together. This design gave me a much better middle ground.

What I wanted to keep from my old site

This was not just a theme swap.

A big part of this redesign was making the new design work with the things I had already built into the site and not throwing away the parts that made it mine.

So while the visual design changed a lot, I kept a number of things from my existing setup, including:

  • my OG image generation pipeline
  • Mastodon sharing and discussion features
  • webmentions support
  • Eleventy Image usage in posts and pages
  • the Speedlify score in the footer
  • my existing footer structure and link organization

That part mattered a lot to me because I did not want to lose the site-specific work I had already invested in just to chase a nicer layout.

What changed in the redesign

The biggest improvement is probably that the site makes more sense now.

The content feels less siloed and more like part of one connected publishing system. Weekly notes now live in the Notes section where they belong. The homepage does a better job of mixing different types of content together. The site directory is more useful. Search is easier to access. Tags and categories feel more intentional. And pages like Listening, Bookshelf, Projects, and the rest of the “small web” sections feel more at home instead of bolted on.

Some of the newer pieces I especially wanted include:

  • a stronger notes section
  • a better site directory
  • improved search
  • accessible theme controls
  • cleaner tag and category browsing
  • support for richer personal sections like listening, bookshelf, podroll, projects, and more

I also really like that the site now feels more cohesive from page to page. Posts, notes, and supporting pages all feel like they belong to the same site instead of feeling like separate systems wearing the same logo.

What this redesign is really about

More than anything, this redesign is me leaning further into what I want this website to be.

I want CybersecKyle to be a place where I can publish serious cybersecurity writing, but also post smaller notes, personal updates, experiments, recommendations, and weird little corners of my life on the web without it feeling out of place.

I want it to be useful.

I want it to be readable.

I want it to feel like a human built it.

And I want it to be something I enjoy adding to, not just something I maintain.

That is probably the biggest reason I made this change. A good redesign should not just make a site look nicer. It should make it easier to keep showing up and using the site the way you want to use it.

This one feels like it does that.

I also added a style guide and colophon

One thing I wanted with this redesign was a page that explains the look and feel of the site a little more directly, along with some of the stack and design choices behind it.

So I added a Style Guide, which also works a bit like a colophon. It gives a quick look at the typography, palette, components, and some of the thinking behind the site.

I always enjoy seeing pages like that on other personal websites, so it felt right to add one here too.

Final thoughts

This redesign probably will not be the final version of CybersecKyle, and that is okay.

Personal websites are supposed to evolve. That is part of what makes them interesting.

But this version feels a lot closer to the site I have been trying to build for a while now. It feels more like a home for the different kinds of things I want to publish, and it feels a lot more aligned with the independent web direction I want to keep leaning into.

So yes, the site has a new look.

But more importantly, it feels more like itself now.
